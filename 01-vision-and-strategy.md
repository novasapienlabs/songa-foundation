# 1. Vision and strategy

Songa is building a scalable community franchise to revillage the world, and the SONGA.LIVE House at the Sundance Film Festival in Boulder, January 19 to 31, 2027, is its first big public moment.

## Songa in one paragraph

*Status: draft · Sources: Songa foundation; Krister, Nov 18-19 talks*

Songa is an immersive experience where patrons and artists co-create as peers. It is also a community, a technology platform, a media house and a real estate play. People come for the feeling of wonder, awe and grace when a room goes quiet and a song is born. The book, the experience and the pitch are different doors into the same story.

## Why Songa exists

*Status: draft · Sources: Songa foundation; Krister, Nov 18 and Nov 23*

- **For artists:** the nonprofit's mission is to create sustainable income for artists. Sponsorship proceeds go to performing artists [Songa foundation].
- **For everyone else:** Songa invites people to find the creator in themselves. The line is now "where we awaken the Creator within", with Creator capitalised [Krister, Nov 23 call].
- **For places:** Songa wants to bring back the village, the sense of belonging that comes from making things together. The tagline is "a scalable community franchise to revillage the world" [Joshua, Nov 19 decision].

## What Songa is building toward

*Status: draft · Sources: Songa foundation; Krister, Nov 18-19 talks*

- **The model:** "Star Wars meets Starbucks." A media franchise of stories and characters, joined to a location franchise of places you can walk into.
- **The category:** location-based entertainment that can be franchised, in the way escape rooms are. Songa calls itself "the escape room you'll never want to escape from."
- **The home format:** patrons and artists can take the format home and turn a house into a co-creative space, called "a Songa."
- **The pieces:** the technology platform, the writers' room, the characters, BOULDER.LIVE, a short film, a book and a building. Each is in alpha or beta [Krister, Nov 19].
- **The brand stance:** Songa wants to be close to an open-source brand. It names no founders, and its characters are archetypes of the people a community needs [Songa foundation].

## Where Songa stands today

*Status: draft · Sources: Krister, Nov 18-19 talks; Joshua, Nov 23*

- **Built quietly:** the work began about ten years ago. Krister's phrase is "an overnight success, ten years in the making."
- **Fragmented by growth:** the story lives across more than a dozen URLs (Krister has spoken of 30 or more sites) and many creative voices. History and goals don't always line up yet [Joshua, Nov 23].
- **Strong on art, lighter on structure:** the creative engine is rich. The business structure and cash-flow model that make it sustainable are still being built [Joshua, Nov 23].
- **Momentum:** Krister reported a request for the sponsor packages and said Sundance Institute will funnel lead flow to Songa [Krister, email, Nov 18].
- **Riverside:** a second, larger space (about 800 people) is back in play. Krister needs a decision by Dec 15 and put it at 49% on Nov 23. Someone in the Davos and social-impact community may help with an investor and donor deck for it [Krister, Nov 23 call].

## How Songa makes money

*Status: needs input · Sources: Sponsorship and pricing, Nov 23; Krister, Nov 18; earlier producer review*

The lines we know of:

- Credentials for individuals (Single Day, First Half, Second Half, All 12 Days). They work like members-only access, so local Boulder people can later become monthly members at a different price [Krister, Nov 23 call].
- Partner packages from $2.5K to $100K and up, with a Patron tier that carries membership past the Sundance Film Festival [Krister, Nov 23 call].
- Donors who pay and house artists.
- Investors, through an idea Krister calls the Patron's Pledge (see Glossary).
- Hosting other organizations' events, turnkey.

Krister's aim for the festival is to cover costs and do something amazing: "in the worst case, we lose money", and people pay more to be part of it next year [Krister, Nov 23 call].

An earlier review estimated year-one revenue between about $285K and $1.15M, and noted the first year may not cover its costs [earlier producer review, inferred].
